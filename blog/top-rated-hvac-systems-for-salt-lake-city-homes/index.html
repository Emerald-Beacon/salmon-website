Top-Rated HVAC Systems for Salt Lake City Homes

Your 2026 guide to the top-rated HVAC systems for Salt Lake City homes — SEER2 ratings, the new A2L refrigerants, real install costs, and the Utah rebates that still cut your price.

Salmon HVAC
Published January 21, 2025  •  Updated September 3, 2026  •  8 min read

For 2026, the top-rated HVAC systems for Salt Lake City homes come from Daikin, Carrier, Lennox, Trane, and Rheem. The best pick for our climate pairs a variable-speed, high-efficiency air conditioner or heat pump (look for 15+ SEER2) with a 90%+ AFUE furnace, runs on one of the new low-GWP A2L refrigerants (R-454B or R-32), and is sized correctly for your home. Expect a full furnace-plus-AC replacement to land in the $7,500–$12,500 range professionally installed, with high-efficiency and heat-pump systems reaching higher. Below we break down the brands, what actually changed for 2026, real Salt Lake City pricing, and how to still cut thousands off the cost with Utah utility rebates.

Why the Right HVAC System Matters in Salt Lake City

Salt Lake City's climate is genuinely demanding on equipment: hot, dry summers that push 100°F, cold snowy winters, high altitude that affects combustion and airflow, and low humidity year-round. Add in the Wasatch Front's winter inversions and summer wildfire smoke, and your HVAC system is doing double duty as your home's air-quality control. A system that's correctly matched to that climate keeps you comfortable, keeps your Rocky Mountain Power and Enbridge Gas (formerly Dominion) bills down, and lasts 15–20 years instead of failing early.

Key Takeaways

  • Trusted brands: Daikin, Carrier, Lennox, Trane, and Rheem are the top-rated systems for Salt Lake City in 2026 — all offer models built for extreme swings and dry air.
  • New efficiency rules: SEER2 (not the old SEER) is now the standard, and every new system installed in 2026 uses a low-GWP A2L refrigerant like R-454B or R-32.
  • Right-sizing beats brand: A correctly sized, professionally installed mid-tier system outperforms an oversized premium one. Insist on a Manual J load calculation.
  • The 25C federal tax credit expired at the end of 2025, but Utah utility rebates are still active in 2026 and can knock $3,500+ off a qualifying heat pump.

What Changed for 2026 (Read This Before You Buy)

If you last shopped for an HVAC system a few years ago, three big things are different now. Knowing them will keep a salesperson from talking past you.

1. SEER2 replaced SEER

As of 2023, cooling efficiency is measured in SEER2, a stricter test that uses higher static pressure to better reflect real ductwork. A unit that was "14 SEER" under the old test is roughly "13.4 SEER2" now — the number got smaller even though the equipment didn't change. Utah sits in the federal Northern region, where the 2026 minimum for split-system air conditioners is 13.4 SEER2. For meaningful summer savings in our heat, we recommend targeting 15–16 SEER2 or higher. On the heating side, look for a furnace AFUE of 90% or better (95%+ for the best gas efficiency).

2. New A2L refrigerants (R-454B and R-32)

The old R-410A refrigerant has been phased out for new equipment. Systems built in 2025 and later use lower-global-warming-potential A2L refrigerants — most commonly R-454B (used by Carrier, Trane, Lennox, and Rheem) or R-32 (used by Daikin). These cut refrigerant climate impact by roughly 75% and are the standard going forward. If you're comparing quotes, any new 2026 install should be on an A2L refrigerant — we walk through what that means for repairs and future service in our R-410A to R-454B refrigerant change guide for Utah homeowners.

3. The federal 25C tax credit ended — but Utah rebates didn't

The federal 25C Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit (up to $2,000 for a qualifying heat pump) expired on December 31, 2025 under the 2025 tax law and is no longer available for 2026 installations. The good news for Northern Utah: local utility rebates are still very much alive. We cover the full, current picture in our 2026 Utah heat pump rebates & tax credits guide — see the rebate section below for the short version.

Top HVAC Systems for Salt Lake City Homes (2026)

1. Daikin

Daikin is the world's largest HVAC manufacturer and a strong all-around choice for our climate — it's also the brand Salmon HVAC is factory-authorized to install as a Daikin Comfort Pro. Daikin's inverter-driven heat pumps and air conditioners modulate output continuously, which is exactly what you want for Salt Lake's wild day-to-night temperature swings. Their 2026 lineup runs on R-32 refrigerant.

  • Excellent inverter/variable-speed performance for consistent comfort.
  • High SEER2 ratings and strong cold-climate heat-pump options.
  • Industry-leading warranties with responsive support.

2. Carrier

Carrier's Infinity® series is a premium option built around precise temperature and humidity control — a real advantage in our dry air. Variable-speed compressors let the system run long, low, efficient cycles instead of blasting on and off.

  • Variable-speed compressors that fine-tune output for efficiency.
  • Superior humidity management for dry Salt Lake City air.
  • ENERGY STAR® certified high-efficiency models.

3. Lennox

Lennox consistently posts some of the highest efficiency numbers in the industry, with select variable-capacity systems reaching into the low-20s SEER2. If your priority is squeezing utility bills to the minimum, Lennox belongs on your shortlist.

  • Category-leading SEER2 ratings on premium models.
  • Advanced filtration that pairs well with our inversion/wildfire-smoke air-quality needs.
  • Full smart-home and zoning compatibility.

4. Trane

Trane's reputation is built on durability — their equipment is known for surviving harsh conditions, which matters through a Wasatch Front winter. Trane also offers dual-fuel setups that pair a heat pump with a gas furnace, an ideal configuration for our cold snaps and one that qualifies for the largest Utah rebates.

  • Rugged, long-lifespan construction.
  • Strong dual-fuel (heat pump + furnace) options for cold-climate efficiency.
  • Reliable variable-speed performance and solid warranties.

5. Rheem

Rheem delivers dependable, energy-efficient systems at a friendlier price point — a smart choice when you want a durable ENERGY STAR® system without premium-tier pricing.

  • High-efficiency options that meet ENERGY STAR® standards.
  • EcoNet™ smart-home integration for remote control.
  • Durable build that handles Salt Lake's extremes.

What a New System Actually Costs in Salt Lake City (2026)

Pricing depends on home size, efficiency tier, system type, and whether your ductwork needs work — but here are realistic 2026 installed ranges to budget against:

  • Full HVAC replacement (furnace + AC): most homeowners land between $7,500 and $12,500; the national range spans roughly $5,000 to $28,000 for large or high-efficiency jobs.
  • Central air conditioner (replacement): about $3,900–$7,900 installed.
  • Gas furnace (replacement): roughly $3,800–$10,000 depending on efficiency and size.
  • Air-source heat pump: generally $6,000–$25,000; a typical residential install falls in the middle of that range.
  • Add-ons: new or replacement ductwork can add ~$3,000+, and correcting undersized returns is common in older Salt Lake homes.

For a deeper local breakdown, see our Utah HVAC replacement cost guide for 2026. If you're deciding between a heat pump and a traditional AC-plus-furnace setup, our 10-year cost comparison runs the numbers for Utah.

Key Features to Look For

  1. Efficiency (SEER2 & AFUE): target 15+ SEER2 for cooling and 90%+ AFUE for heating in our climate.
  2. Variable-speed / inverter operation: the single biggest comfort upgrade for Salt Lake's temperature swings — long, quiet, efficient cycles instead of on/off blasts.
  3. Humidity and air-quality control: our dry winters benefit from whole-home humidification, and inversion/wildfire season makes good filtration (MERV 13 or an add-on air cleaner) worthwhile.
  4. Smart thermostat compatibility: Nest and Ecobee both qualify for small Utah utility rebates and cut runtime — see whether smart thermostats are worth it.
  5. Strong warranty + local service: a great warranty means little without a nearby company to honor it. Confirm parts and labor coverage and who services it.

How to Choose the Right System for Your Home

Get a proper load calculation

Size is where most installs go wrong. A unit that's too small can't keep up on a 100°F afternoon; an oversized one short-cycles, wastes energy, and never dehumidifies properly. A quality contractor runs a Manual J load calculation based on your square footage, insulation, windows, and layout — not a rule-of-thumb guess. Multi-story homes often benefit from zoning for even comfort upstairs and down.

Insist on professional installation

Even the best equipment underperforms if it's installed poorly. Work with a licensed Utah contractor who knows local building stock and altitude derating. Proper installation prevents air leaks, refrigerant charge errors, and the ductwork problems that quietly waste 20–30% of your system's output. Salmon HVAC handles AC installation, furnace installation, and heat pump systems across Salt Lake City and the surrounding Wasatch Front.

Plan for maintenance

Schedule a tune-up twice a year — before cooling season and before heating season. Simple upkeep like filter changes and a refrigerant/charge check keeps efficiency high and extends system life well past the 15-year mark.

Utah Rebates & Financing That Lower Your 2026 Cost

Even without the expired federal 25C credit, Salt Lake City homeowners can still stack meaningful savings in 2026:

  • Rocky Mountain Power Wattsmart® Homes: up to $1,700 for a qualifying air-source heat pump and up to $2,000 for a dual-fuel, ductless, or ground-source heat pump, plus up to $100 for a qualifying smart thermostat. Applications typically go in within 90 days of installation.
  • Enbridge Gas (ThermWise®): rebates on qualifying high-efficiency furnaces by AFUE rating, plus $50–$75 for smart thermostats and weatherization measures like attic insulation and air sealing.
  • Manufacturer & contractor promotions: combined with utility rebates, total savings on a qualifying heat pump can reach $3,500 or more.
  • Financing: Salmon HVAC offers financing so you can install an efficient system now and pay monthly. See our Utah HVAC financing breakdown or apply through our financing page.

Rebate amounts and eligibility change, so confirm current figures with your utility (or ask us) before you buy.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What is the best HVAC system for Salt Lake City homes in 2026?
A: Daikin, Carrier, Lennox, Trane, and Rheem are the top-rated brands for our climate. The best system is one that's correctly sized, rated 15+ SEER2 for cooling and 90%+ AFUE for heating, uses a modern A2L refrigerant, and is professionally installed — brand matters less than proper sizing and installation.

Q: What is SEER2 and how is it different from SEER?
A: SEER2 is the current cooling-efficiency standard that replaced SEER in 2023. It uses a stricter test (higher external static pressure) that better reflects real home ductwork, so the numbers run slightly lower than old SEER ratings for the same equipment. Utah's Northern-region minimum for split-system ACs is 13.4 SEER2; we recommend 15–16 SEER2 or higher.

Q: Do new HVAC systems use a different refrigerant now?
A: Yes. R-410A has been phased out for new equipment. Systems built in 2025 and later use lower-GWP A2L refrigerants — most commonly R-454B or R-32 — which cut refrigerant climate impact by about 75%.

Q: Is there still a federal tax credit for a new HVAC system in 2026?
A: No. The federal 25C Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit expired on December 31, 2025 and is not available for 2026 installations. However, Utah utility rebates through Rocky Mountain Power's Wattsmart and Enbridge Gas's ThermWise programs are still active and can save you thousands.

Q: What's the average lifespan of an HVAC system?
A: Most systems last 15–20 years with proper maintenance. Twice-yearly tune-ups, timely repairs, and regular filter changes are what push a system toward the top of that range.
