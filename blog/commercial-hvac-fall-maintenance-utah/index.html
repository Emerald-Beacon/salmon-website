Commercial HVAC Fall Maintenance for Northern Utah Businesses (RTU + VRF)

The rooftop units and VRF systems that cooled your building all summer are about to switch jobs. Fall is the short window to make sure they can — before the first cold snap tests your heat for you. This guide walks Northern Utah facility managers through the pre-season checklist that prevents no-heat emergencies, controls energy costs, and keeps your building open all winter.

Salmon HVAC
Published August 4, 2026  •  10 min read
Quick Answer
  • Schedule commercial fall maintenance in September through early October, before Northern Utah's first hard freeze — while moderate weather still lets a tech test both heating and cooling.
  • The visit that matters most is the heating changeover: heat exchanger inspection, ignition and gas checks, and economizer calibration so a stuck damper doesn't freeze coils or waste gas all winter.
  • Most commercial systems need at least two professional visits a year (spring and fall); high-use buildings often go quarterly.
  • Planned fall service is far cheaper than a no-heat emergency during a cold snap — and it protects the equipment you're not ready to replace yet.

For a Northern Utah business, heat isn't a convenience in January — it's a condition of staying open. A restaurant can't seat a cold dining room, a retail floor empties out when customers keep their coats on, and an office with a dead rooftop unit sends everyone home. Yet the failures that shut buildings down in December are almost always visible in September, when there's still time to fix them on your schedule instead of the weather's.

Salmon HVAC has kept Northern Utah comfortable since 1979, and we maintain and repair commercial rooftop units (RTUs) and VRF systems on offices, retail centers, restaurants, dealerships, and industrial buildings across Centerville, Bountiful, Layton, Ogden, and Salt Lake City. This is the fall pre-season checklist we actually run on a roof — what to inspect, why it matters here, and how to get it done without disrupting your operation.

Why Fall Is the Critical Window for Commercial HVAC

Fall maintenance isn't just "another visit." It's the one service call each year that has to catch a system in the act of switching from cooling to heating, and it works because the timing is right:

  • Moderate weather lets a tech test everything. In September a technician can run the cooling side one hour and fire the heat the next, in the same visit — impossible in a July heat wave or a January freeze.
  • It's proactive, not reactive. Small issues — a weak igniter, a dirty flame sensor, a sticky economizer damper — get corrected before they become a closed building.
  • It protects your energy budget. Heating season is when commercial gas and electric bills spike; a clean, calibrated system going into winter runs measurably cheaper than a neglected one.
  • You get a scheduled window. Book ahead and you pick the time. Wait for a failure and you're competing with every other business for emergency dispatch during the first cold snap.

In Northern Utah, the first hard freeze typically lands in early-to-mid October, so the practical deadline is to have commercial fall maintenance done by the end of September or the first week of October. The Commercial Fall Maintenance Checklist (RTU + VRF)

Below is the core scope of a thorough commercial fall visit. Some items apply to packaged rooftop units, some to VRF, and many to both — a good technician tailors the list to your specific equipment.

1. Heating changeover and combustion safety (RTUs)

This is the heart of fall service on a gas-heat rooftop unit. The technician should:

  • Inspect the heat exchanger for cracks or corrosion — a cracked exchanger is a carbon-monoxide risk and a hard stop.
  • Clean and test the burners, igniter, and flame sensor, and check the flue and combustion-air passages.
  • Verify gas pressure and the gas valve, and confirm the unit lights reliably and holds flame.
  • Test safety controls — high-limit switches, rollout switches, and pressure switches.

Most winter no-heat calls trace directly back to one of these components. Catching them now is the difference between a scheduled part swap and a Saturday-night emergency.

2. Economizer testing and calibration

The economizer is the single most overlooked — and most expensive — item on a commercial roof. It pulls in cool outside air for "free cooling," but in winter a damper stuck open floods the unit with freezing air. The result can be frozen coils, wasted heat, and a gas bill that quietly doubles. Fall service should:

  • Confirm the outside-air damper opens fully and, just as important, closes fully.
  • Check the actuator and linkage for wear and correct stroke.
  • Calibrate the changeover setpoint so the economizer enables at the right outdoor temperature or enthalpy.
  • Verify the minimum outside-air position won't create freeze conditions during occupied hours.

3. Coils, filters, and airflow

  • Inspect and clean condenser and evaporator coils loaded up by a summer of Northern Utah dust.
  • Replace filters and confirm the right MERV rating for the space — poor filtration strains the system and hurts indoor air quality. (See our Utah air filter replacement guide for sizing and ratings.)
  • Check belts, pulleys, and fan motor bearings; replace worn belts before they snap in the cold.
  • Clear outside-air inlet screens of leaves and debris.

4. Controls, thermostats, and scheduling

  • Confirm every thermostat and sensor reads accurately — mismatched setpoints can run heating and cooling against each other and waste money.
  • Reset occupied/unoccupied schedules for winter hours and any holiday closures.
  • Verify building automation (BAS) integration and alarms are reporting correctly.

5. VRF-specific fall checks

If your building runs a Variable Refrigerant Flow system instead of, or alongside, rooftop units, the fall scope shifts:

  • Verify heating-mode operation and, on heat-recovery systems, that simultaneous heating and cooling zones are transferring energy correctly.
  • Check refrigerant charge and piping for leaks, and inspect branch controllers.
  • Clean indoor-unit filters and confirm zone controls and schedules.
  • Confirm outdoor-unit defrost operation ahead of freezing temperatures.

For a full breakdown of how these systems work and what they need, see our complete guide to VRF systems for Utah and our page on VRF system maintenance & repair in Utah.

6. Drainage, freeze protection, and the physical unit

  • Clear and treat condensate pans and drain lines so they don't freeze and back up.
  • Check any heat tape or freeze protection on exposed lines.
  • Inspect the cabinet, panels, curb, and seals for rust, gaps, or roof-penetration leaks that let weather in.
  • Lubricate moving parts and tighten electrical connections that vibrate loose over a season of runtime.

What Skipping Fall Maintenance Actually Costs

Deferring a few hundred dollars of preventive service feels like a saving until the numbers come due in December. Running commercial equipment into winter without a fall check-up tends to cost more in four ways:

Skipped itemWhat it can cost later
Heat exchanger / ignition not checkedNo-heat emergency dispatch, after-hours premiums, a closed or freezing building
Economizer not calibratedFrozen coil, wasted heat, and gas bills that climb quietly all winter
Coils and filters left dirtyHigher energy use, strained compressors, shorter equipment life
Condensate drain not clearedFrozen backup, water damage, and shutdowns

The most expensive line item is almost always downtime. Every hour a restaurant, store, or office is closed or unworkable costs revenue that dwarfs the price of the maintenance visit that would have prevented it. Planned service converts an unpredictable emergency into a scheduled, budgeted line item.

Maintenance Agreements: Why Most Businesses Don't Do This Per-Visit

For a building with multiple units, one-off service calls are the hard way to do this. A commercial maintenance agreement bundles your spring and fall visits, usually at a lower per-visit rate, and adds real operational value:

  • Priority service. Agreement customers go to the front of the line when a cold snap swamps every contractor in the valley.
  • Documented equipment history. Age, refrigerant, repairs, and condition tracked per unit — so repair-vs-replace decisions are based on data, not guesses.
  • Predictable budgeting. Scheduled visits and known pricing instead of surprise emergency invoices.
  • Longer equipment life. Consistent maintenance is the single biggest factor in reaching the top of a unit's lifespan range instead of the bottom.

Efficiency Incentives Worth Checking

Fall is also a good moment to look at whether an aging unit should be replaced rather than repaired — and whether that replacement qualifies for a utility incentive. Programs such as Rocky Mountain Power's Wattsmart Business offerings have historically provided rebates for qualifying high-efficiency commercial HVAC equipment and controls in Utah. Program details and amounts change from year to year, so we help identify what's currently available and applicable before you commit to a project. If one or more of your units is in the 13–18-year range, pairing a fall assessment with an incentive check often makes the timing of a planned replacement work in your favor.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should a Northern Utah business schedule commercial HVAC fall maintenance?

Book it for September through early October, before the first hard cold snap — which typically arrives in early-to-mid October in Northern Utah. Fall's moderate temperatures let a technician test both heating and cooling in a single visit, and scheduling ahead means you get a service window instead of waiting on emergency dispatch when your heat quits.

How often should commercial rooftop units and VRF systems be serviced?

At least twice a year — once in spring before cooling season and once in fall before heating season. High-use buildings like restaurants, retail, and 24/7 operations often benefit from quarterly service. Filters are typically checked or changed more often, sometimes monthly, depending on the environment.

Why does the economizer matter during fall maintenance?

The economizer uses cool outside air for free cooling, but a damper stuck open in winter pulls in freezing air that can freeze coils, waste heating energy, and drive up gas bills. Fall is when a technician verifies the damper opens and closes fully, calibrates the changeover point, and confirms the minimum outside-air position won't create freeze conditions.

What does commercial HVAC preventive maintenance cost in Utah?

Per-visit commercial maintenance in Northern Utah commonly runs a few hundred dollars per rooftop unit, scaling by unit count, tonnage, and system type. Most businesses use a maintenance agreement that bundles scheduled visits at a lower per-visit rate and adds priority service. We provide a fixed quote after reviewing your equipment.

Does fall maintenance help with a commercial no-heat emergency?

Yes. Most winter no-heat failures trace back to issues visible in the fall — cracked or dirty heat exchangers, weak igniters and flame sensors, failing gas valves, and clogged filters straining the system. Catching them during a planned visit is far cheaper than emergency dispatch during a cold snap, and it prevents the downtime and lost revenue that come with a closed building.
